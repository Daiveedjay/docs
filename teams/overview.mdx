A team gives several people one shared place for their project rules and for a record of what Iris Code found. Everyone checks code against the same standard, and the results from editors, CI and cloud scans collect in one history.

Creating a team is free. A Free Team shares a monthly allowance of local project actions and cloud scans, within a limit on how many collaborators and projects can use it. Paid seats add Pro features for the people who hold them.

Three things are separate, and it helps to keep them apart:

- **Membership** means you belong to the team and can see its records.
- **Active Free capacity** decides who can spend the team's Free allowance, and on which projects.
- **A paid seat** gives that person the Pro features that apply.

## What a team is

A team owns everything that belongs to it: its members and their seats, its connected repositories, its published standard, its record of results and its billing.

You can own up to **three** teams. Most people need one.

## Members and roles

| Role | Can do |
| --- | --- |
| **Owner** | Everything, including billing, seat changes and deleting the team. Exactly one, and cannot be removed or demoted |
| **Admin** | Invite and remove members, connect repositories, publish the standard |
| **Member** | Use everything in the team. Cannot invite, remove, or change billing |

The owner cannot be demoted or removed. Otherwise a team could end up with nobody able to manage it.

## Inviting people

Invite someone by email address and choose their role. The invitation is created even if the email fails to arrive, and the screen tells you which of the two happened.

A pending invitation holds a place until it is answered: collaborator capacity on a Free Team, or a seat on a paid team. Invitations expire after **7 days**, and revoking one frees its place. **Members** shows how much room you have.

<Note>
  Iris Code reports each invitation separately. If you send five invitations with two free places, two succeed and three fail, and you are told which three.
</Note>

## What every member gets

A paid seat gives that person the Pro features that apply, through that team. Free collaborators who are active on the team can spend the team's own monthly allowance on its active projects.

Neither one moves a personal allowance into the team. See [Team entitlement](/teams/entitlement) for how team and personal access fit together.

## The shared standard

A team publishes one standard: a `.irisconfig.json` file, the settings file that tells Iris Code what to check and how strictly. You build and preview it in Config Studio.

The file is committed beside your code like any other config. A new machine therefore applies the standard on its first push, with nothing extra to set up.

Publishing does not change any repository. It records what the team expects, while the config file inside each repository is what actually runs. Every report names the version of the standard it used, so you can see when a repository has drifted from it. Iris Code shows the difference rather than correcting it for you.

## The audit trail

The audit trail is the team's record of results. Every completed audit adds an entry with:

- what ran, and on which commit and branch
- the score
- how many findings were open, and how many were high severity
- which version of the standard applied

Entries come from three places, and all three land in the same trail:

- **The editor**, on every completed team scan
- **CI**, from `iris gate`, when the repository sets a `teamProjectId`
- **Cloud scans**, from connected GitHub repositories

This record holds counts and commit identifiers only. It contains no source code, file names or finding text.

Connected cloud repositories also keep their own, more detailed scan reports, which do include file paths and finding locations. See [cloud reports and retention](/cloud/overview#reports-and-retention).

## Cloud scans for a team

A team has its own connected repositories and its own cloud scan allowance, completely separate from any member's personal repositories.

- Free Teams share one monthly pool.
- Paid teams get a separate allocation that grows with the number of seats. The [pricing page](https://iriscode.co/pricing) shows the current paid quantities.

New repositories start in manual mode, so nothing is scanned automatically until an owner opts in. Automatic scans then run within the allowance that applies. See [Scan allowances](/cloud/allowances).

## Trials

A team can start a **14-day trial** with 3 seats, no card required.

<Warning>
A new team always starts on Free. An eligible owner or admin has to start the optional 14-day trial on purpose. Creating another team does not make you eligible for a new trial, and does not refill a Free allowance. The Members and Billing screens show where the team stands.
</Warning>

## Deleting a team

Only the owner can delete a team, and only after the subscription has been cancelled. Deleting a team does not tell the billing provider anything, so a team removed mid-subscription would keep being charged for something that no longer exists.

Deleting removes members, invitations, connected repositories, cloud reports and the audit trail. A record that the deletion happened is kept. Deleting your last team returns you to your personal account.

Read [Team governance](/teams/governance) for controls over coding agents, behaviour when offline and records of pre-push checks.
