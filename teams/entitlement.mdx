A team seat gives you Pro in your editor and command line, plus access to that team?s repositories and reports. A personal Pro subscription covers your own cloud repositories separately. This page explains which subscription provides your access and what happens when it ends.

## How Pro is decided

Iris Code checks three things in order and stops at the first that grants it:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Personal Pro subscription">
    An active personal subscription. Reported as **individual**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Personal Pro trial">
    An active 14-day Pro trial. Also reported as **individual**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Active team seat">
    Reported as **team**, along with which team is covering you.
  </Step>
</Steps>

A seat is checked **last**, and only when nothing personal already grants Pro. So joining a team never changes anything for someone who already pays for Pro.

## What a seat gives you

Everything in Pro, in your editor and CLI, for as long as the team's subscription runs. Your account page names the team covering you and the date its cover currently runs to.

It also gives you access to that team: its connected repositories, its reports, its audit trail, and its scan allowance.

## What a seat does not give you

**Cloud scans of your own repositories.** Your account and each team have separate scan allowances. A team seat does not provide scans for your personal account.

If you want your own side projects audited in the cloud, that needs your own Pro subscription. The seat covers the team's repositories only.

<Warning>
  This is the reason Iris Code does not cancel a personal subscription when you join a team, and does not ask you to. Cancelling would silently stop your own repositories being audited, which is not something a team seat covers.
</Warning>

## If you have both

Your editor is covered by your personal subscription, and the seat is what gives you the team. Iris Code shows a notice on your billing screen saying you are covered twice, so nobody finds out from a bank statement.

Keeping both is reasonable if you work on your own repositories as well as the team's. If you only ever work on the team's, cancelling your personal subscription stops that charge without affecting your access to the team at all.

## Two teams

Only one seat provides your editor entitlement, and a **paid** team always beats a trialling one. That matters because a trial expires in a fortnight while a subscription does not, so picking the paid one means your Pro lasts as long as somebody is actually paying for it.

Scan allowances are different: you get both teams' pools, each spendable on that team's own repositories.

## When cover ends

### You are removed from the team

Immediately. Iris Code tells you as it happens rather than leaving you to discover it, and returns you to your personal account. If your Pro came from that seat it ends with it; if you pay for Pro yourself, nothing changes.

That team's notifications are cleared from your inbox at the same time, because every link in them pointed somewhere you can no longer open.

### The team stops paying

A 7-day grace window keeps every member on Pro. After it, the entitlement becomes read-only and members drop to Free together. Existing reports stay readable. See [Seats and billing](/teams/seats-and-billing).

### The team is deleted

Cover ends with it, and its repositories, reports and audit trail are removed. Your personal account is untouched. Deleting your last team returns you to it.

### Your seat is deactivated but you stay a member

A seat that is not active does not grant Pro. Ask an owner or admin to reactivate it; the team's seat allowance has to have room.

## What your account page shows

Your billing screen distinguishes the three cases rather than reading your own subscription alone:

- **Paying yourself** - your plan, price and renewal date, plus the "covered twice" notice if you also hold a seat
- **Covered by a team** - which team, until when, that there is nothing for you to pay, and that a personal subscription is what would audit your own repositories
- **Neither** - the Free plan, with the upgrade path

<Note>
  Your editor and the website resolve Pro through the same logic, so they cannot disagree about whether you have it.
</Note>
